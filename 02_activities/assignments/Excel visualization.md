### Excel visualization

* What software did you use to create your data visualization?
  * I used Microsoft Excel to create this data visualization. Data source: [https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/monthly-communicable-disease-surveillance-data/]
* Who is your intended audience? 
  * The intended audience is the general public and also public health policymakers. This is of a presentation purpose to show the trajectory of influenza and COVID-19 within Toronto.
* What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 
  * The main purpose is to present a point, and potentially correct a misconception, that the "disappearance" of influenza during COVID-19 times was rather a system failure than a biological fact. While the chart appears to show a negative correlation between influenza and COVID-19, the deeper message is that pandemic systems are finite and limited. The drop in influenza in 2020 and 2021 conveys that the healthcare system was so overloaded by COVID-19 (which can be inferred from the secondary y axis scale on the right) that it effectively stopped counting other diseases.
* What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 
  * I chose a multi-line plot to allow direct comparison of the two trends on the same temporal scale, although COVID-19 data is only available from 2020 to 2024. By placing both lines in one plot, I reduced the cognitive load on the viewer, allowing them to directly compare the two rather than looking at two separate charts.
  * I plotted the YTD rates of influenza and COVID-19 on two y axes because their ranges are very different. I also added an explanatory footnote as annotation to the 2020-2021 gap explaining why the number dropped. Without this, the deign might mislead the audience into thinking influenza vanished during COVID-19.
* How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 
  * I manually created the visualization in Excel and set color, font, and other aesthetic settings, which are not very reproducible. If another person opens my file on a different version of Excel or on a different device, the rendered plot would look different.
* How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  
  * I used blue and orange as colors of influenza and COVID-19 lines against a white background. These two colors are complementary and opposite colors on the color wheel that create maximum visual contrast. I chose Arial font with at least a font size of 12 (except the footnote which is 10) to ensure texts are more accessible.
  * To make this more accessible to screen readers, I could provide an alt-text description: "A line chart showing a spike in COVID-19 cases and a simultaneous drop in influenza cases. Note: The influenza drop represents unrecorded cases due to system overload, not a lack of infections."
* Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  
  * The individuals who had influenza in 2020-2021 but were not recorded are the most likely impacted. The visualization acknowledges their disappearance from the database due to system overload. It might also impact healthcare workers as a testament to their labour capacity. The spike in COVID cases and drop in influenza cases is a visual footprint of their burnout and exhaustion of hospital resources.
* How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 
  * I included both diseases to show the relationship/trade-off between influenza and COVID-19. I excluded monthly data and focused on YTD rates so the plots would not be too busy. The data itself excluded influenza cases during the surge, which is exactly what I wanted to visualize. Similar to the Python visualization, the demographic details, or outcome severities were not available in the dataset, so there are unseen data that are not included in the visualization as well.
* What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
  * The underwater labour includes many aspects, such as data collection (the public health workers and hospital staff who conducted tests and reported case data), infrastructure (the City of Toronto IT support team who maintains the Open Portal database), and data preprocessing (me extracting and consolidating data from database files of consecutive years). Especially for data collection, the missing data points for influenza represent the excess clinical labor of hospital staff who were too busy saving lives to reporting other disease cases.
